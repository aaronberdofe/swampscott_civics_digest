# Swampscott Master Plan 2035: Condensed Reference for Meeting Summaries

**Source:** *Swampscott Master Plan 2035: Learning from the Past to Shape the Future, Final Master Plan Report* (232 pages), prepared by the Metropolitan Area Planning Council (MAPC) for the Town of Swampscott. Transmitted to the Town May 2026; adoption by the Planning Board and Select Board at a joint session was anticipated for June 17, 2026 (verify adoption status in those boards' minutes).

**Page numbers below are the plan's printed page numbers.** IDs follow the plan's own scheme: goals `XX-G##`, strategies `XX-S###`, recommended actions `A-001` to `A-247`.

## How to use this file when summarizing meetings

- Connect a meeting item to the plan only when the topic clearly matches a goal, strategy or action below. Name the ID (e.g., "relates to LU-S041, recodifying the zoning bylaw") so readers can check it.
- The plan is **non-binding guidance**. Never say a board "must" or "is required to" follow it. Do not claim a board's vote implements the plan unless the meeting record says so.
- Do not invent plan content. This file covers the vision, all 42 goals, the 25 high-priority strategies shown in the plan's at-a-glance chart, and the Appendix B action list by lead implementer. Strategy details, resource estimates and partner lists are in the full plan (implementation tables pp. 125-151; exhaustive goals and strategies pp. 152-212).
- Note when an item is a **short-term (2026-2028)** action, since those are the plan's near-term priorities.

## 1. Plan at a glance

- **Horizon:** 2026-2035. Reviews: annual progress review, mid-point assessment around 2029-2030, next full update around 2033-2034 (p. 24).
- **Structure:** 8 elements, **42 goals, 96 strategies, 247 recommended actions**.
- **Priority tiers:** 35 high-priority strategies (act 2026-2028), 38 medium (2029-2032), 22 low (2033-2035+). Actions by timeframe: **76 short-term, 125 mid-term, 46 long-term**.
- **High-priority strategies by element:** Transportation has the most (12), then Public Facilities (6), Land Use & Zoning (5).
- **Master Plan Implementation Committee:** the first recommendation carried over from the 2016-2025 plan is to establish one. The Planning Board voiced support on **January 12, 2026**, suggested topic subcommittees, and cited the Open Space Committee's Airtable-style tracking as a model (p. 8).
- **Process:** 27-member Advisory Group, 2024-2025 process; 385 survey responses (1,828 open comments); three public forums (130-165 participants); six Advisory Group working sessions; public comment on Draft 1.5 (March 2 - April 18, 2026) drew 15 responses.
- **Chapter start pages:** Economic & Community Development p. 41; Land Use, Zoning & Shaping Growth p. 50; Housing Options & Neighborhoods p. 62; Transportation & Mobility p. 73; Open Space, Recreation & Natural Environment p. 83; Public Facilities & Services p. 95; Historical & Cultural Resources p. 106; Climate, Sustainability, Energy & Hazard Mitigation p. 115.

## 2. Vision 2035 (abbreviated, p. 33)

By 2035 Swampscott will be a thriving, resilient, inclusive coastal community rooted in maritime heritage and natural beauty. Diverse, affordable, climate-ready housing for all ages; welcoming neighborhoods that preserve historic character while embracing carefully planned growth. Vibrant hubs at **Humphrey Street, Vinnin Square, Anthony's Pier 4 and Hadley School** support local businesses and walkable destinations. Leadership in climate adaptation (infrastructure investment, green space, safe multimodal transportation); the **Rail Trail** connects neighborhoods; beaches, parks and facilities are accessible and well maintained. Transparent, collaborative, equitable governance; fiscal stability while investing in schools, public facilities, digital infrastructure and services.

Five vision pillars (pp. 35-36): (1) Preserved & enhanced core identity; (2) Thriving neighborhoods & quality of life (incl. restoring Kings Beach and coastal water quality); (3) Vibrant community hubs & economic vitality; (4) Sustainability, mobility & climate resilience (seawalls, stormwater, Rail Trail, Vision Zero, EV charging); (5) Public spaces & gathering (parks, beaches, a community center), plus Governance & engagement (transparency, fiscal planning, digital communication).

## 3. Town snapshot and key conditions (pp. 27-28, 50, 62-63)

- **Place and people:** 3.05 sq. mi. between Salem and Lynn. Population 15,487 (2023 estimate). Median age 45.3; 65+ is 19.3%, under-18 is 19.4%. The 65+ cohort was projected to grow 64.9% from 2010 to 2030 while school-age population fell 15.8% (MAPC 2016).
- **Income and costs:** median household income $128,964 (ACS 2019-2023); **40% of households are cost-burdened**; median home value $690,800; median gross rent $2,223.
- **Housing stock:** about 6,297 units; 58% single-family, 42% multi-unit; about 73-74% owner-occupied; about 40% built before 1940. Home purchase costs more than doubled 2012-2022.
- **Subsidized Housing Inventory (SHI):** 255 units = **4.07%**, versus the state 10% threshold (626 units). **Elm Place** (120 units, under construction) and **Veterans Crossing** (41 units, Chapter 40B process; a March 2025 citizen petition to halt it failed 114-104; ZBA review continuing) would raise the town to about 416 units (6.6%), a gap of roughly 210 units.
- **Land use:** 87% of zoning is residential but only 65% of assessed land matches; commercial is 4.6% of land use; school enrollment is down 9.4%; Humphrey Street downtown parking was about 62% utilized at peak (2018 study).
- **Zoning and funding milestones:** May 2023 rezoning of Vinnin Square/Essex Street to B-3 and B-4; Vinnin Square Design Guidelines adopted January 2024; **MBTA Communities Section 3A multifamily overlay adopted May 2024** (Essex Street and Vinnin Square districts, capacity about 1,148 units); 2019 Chapter 40R zoning for the 2.3-acre Glover site; accessory dwelling unit (ADU) bylaw approved 2022; Specialized Energy Code adopted 2024; Coastal Flood Area Overlay updates proposed for the 2025 Annual Town Meeting; **Community Preservation Act (CPA) passed November 2024 (1.5% surcharge, effective FY2026)**.

## 4. Projects and initiatives the plan treats as underway (as of spring 2026)

- **Vinnin Square:** Centercorp Retail Properties building a mixed-use redevelopment at the Swampscott Mall site (restaurants and a residential component); National Development proposing multifamily housing on the Glover Estate property that preserves the historic farmhouse.
- **Hawthorne / Anthony's Pier 4:** the Hawthorne Reuse Advisory Committee delivered its final report to the Select Board in November 2025 (mixed-use redevelopment with an oceanfront park element) and was then dissolved.
- **Hadley School reuse:** adaptive reuse project in design development; Delmar Hotels presented plans at a September 2025 public forum.
- **Rail Trail:** extension design about 25% complete; TIP project #610666 is programmed for FFY 2028 construction funding (actions A-042, A-129, A-136).
- **Climate:** Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness (MVP) 2.0 in progress (May 2026); Green Community designation since December 2010; Climate Leader Community certification May 2025.
- **Other:** Community Life Center Feasibility Task Force evaluating sites; Chapter 61/61B proceedings involving Tedesco Country Club being monitored; Resilient Swampscott plan and Hazard Mitigation Plan (2025-2030) guide seawall and stormwater work.
- **Earlier accomplishments cited:** $8.2M Public Safety Building renovation (2022); 2.1 miles of water main replacement; $399,000 MassDOT Complete Streets funding (2021); radar speed signs at ten locations; $75,000 CDBG façade grants to eight businesses; Economic Development Committee created 2020; Phase II historic survey (127 structures, 2021); Certified Local Government status (2020).

## 5. Ten cross-cutting themes (pp. 15-17)

1. Community-wide climate resilience and sustainability
2. Sustainable, walkable, transit-oriented growth (MBTA Communities zoning)
3. Diverse, affordable, climate-adaptable housing
4. Safe, equitable, multimodal mobility
5. Preserve and leverage historic and cultural heritage
6. Resilient public infrastructure and services
7. Vibrant, accessible public spaces (beaches, parks, Rail Trail)
8. Diverse economic development
9. Smart technology and future-oriented planning (EVs, broadband, autonomous vehicles)
10. Collaborative, transparent, equitable governance

## 6. All 42 goals, with high-priority strategies (p. 14)

**Economic & Community Development (ED)**
- Goals: ED-G01 Enhance infrastructure for businesses; ED-G02 Promote a diverse local economy; ED-G03 Support mixed-use development; ED-G04 Explore municipal broadband; ED-G05 Leverage tourism; ED-G06 Financial planning & fiscal stability.
- High-priority: **ED-S011** invest in parking, transportation, broadband & permitting; **ED-S031** mixed-use zoning for Vinnin Square & the commuter rail station; **ED-S061** proactive financial strategies & diverse revenue streams.

**Land Use, Zoning & Shaping Growth (LU)**
- Goals: LU-G01 Balance development & preservation; LU-G02 Promote diverse & affordable housing; LU-G03 Enhance & protect green spaces; LU-G04 Ensure transparent & equitable zoning.
- High-priority: **LU-S011** define neighborhood character (include Indigenous history); **LU-S021** enable "missing middle" & mixed-use housing through zoning; **LU-S022** CPA allocation guidelines (housing, open space, history); **LU-S032** green infrastructure for stormwater; **LU-S041** recodify zoning, streamline permitting, improve transparency.

**Housing Options & Neighborhoods (HO)**
- Goals: HO-G01 Develop a more diverse housing supply; HO-G02 Expand housing choices for all ages & abilities; HO-G03 Renew the aging housing supply; HO-G04 Build local capacity for housing goals.
- High-priority: **HO-S011** encourage infill (ADUs, small multifamily, cluster housing); **HO-S021** age-friendly options & support for seniors downsizing; **HO-S042** build Affordable Housing Trust capacity.

**Transportation & Mobility Options (TR)**
- Goals: TR-G01 Improve roadway safety for all users; TR-G02 Expand pedestrian & bicycle network; TR-G03 Enhance public transit access; TR-G04 Manage traffic & parking; TR-G05 Coordinate land use & transportation; TR-G06 Coordinate regional mobility opportunities; TR-G07 Plan for emerging transportation technology.
- High-priority (the plan's chart shows only 3 of the 12): **TR-S021** complete the Rail Trail with safe connections to key destinations; **TR-S031** funded road/sidewalk maintenance with ADA and low-impact-development (LID) standards; **TR-S121** pedestrian & bicycle safety and traffic calming.

**Open Space, Recreation & Natural Environment (OS)**
- Goals: OS-G01 Enhance & maintain beaches; OS-G02 Expand & connect the Rail Trail; OS-G03 Improve accessibility & inclusivity; OS-G04 More varied recreational opportunities; OS-G05 Prioritize maintenance of existing spaces.
- High-priority: **OS-S011** achieve swimmable beaches (remediate Kings Beach pollution); **OS-S021** expedite the Rail Trail and connect schools, library, train station; **OS-S051** proactive maintenance plan for parks & open spaces.

**Public Facilities & Services (PF)**
- Goals: PF-G01 Maintain & upgrade water & sewer infrastructure; PF-G02 Improve roads, sidewalks & rights-of-way; PF-G03 Ensure capacity of public safety & services; PF-G04 Plan for long-term capital needs; PF-G05 Improve civic communication & digital access; PF-G06 Public facilities upkeep.
- High-priority: **PF-S011** fund seawalls, sewer & stormwater green infrastructure; **PF-S012** enforce sidewalk cleaning and overgrowth rules; **PF-S021** long-term road/sidewalk plan (ADA & LID); **PF-S031** review & improve waste management programs; **PF-S051** expand economic development capacity; **PF-S062** promote a strong educational system.

**Historical & Cultural Resources (HC)**
- Goals: HC-G01 Preserve & protect historic resources; HC-G02 Celebrate & promote cultural identity; HC-G03 Balance preservation with adaptive reuse; HC-G04 Advance equity in cultural representation; HC-G05 Coordinate regional arts & cultural opportunities; HC-G06 Leverage arts & culture for climate resilience awareness.
- High-priority: **HC-S021** proactive system to identify and protect historic properties; **HC-S031** expand Historical Commission capacity.

**Climate, Sustainability, Energy & Hazard Mitigation (CS)**
- Goals: CS-G01 Reduce greenhouse gas emissions; CS-G02 Build coastal & stormwater resilience; CS-G03 Advance renewable energy & efficiency; CS-G04 Embed climate across all plan elements.
- High-priority: **CS-S031** update the Coastal Flood Overlay and climate-resilient zoning; **CS-S042** green infrastructure and nature-based solutions.

## 9. Known quirks in the source

- The Appendix B table lists A-200 under both the Climate Action & Resilience Committee (community workshops) and the Cultural Council (public art on climate themes), and A-247 under both DPW (water conservation education) and Animal Control (wildlife interaction). Treat the ID as ambiguous in those cases and cite the action description.
- The plan gives both "2026-2027 construction" (p. 23) and "FFY 2028 construction funding" (A-129, A-042) for the Rail Trail. Rely on the TIP wording and check current MPO documents for the actual schedule.
- Statistics are mostly ACS 2019-2023 estimates; the plan notes they come from limited data collected early in the 2024-2025 process.
