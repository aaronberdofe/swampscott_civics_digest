# Swampscott Open Space & Recreation Plan Update 2021-2028: Condensed Reference for Meeting Summaries

**Source:** *Open Space & Recreation Plan Update 2021-2028*, Town of Swampscott (141-page PDF with appendices). Prepared by the Open Space & Recreation Plan Committee (OSRPC); written by Molly O'Connell, Senior Planner. Submitted to the state Division of Conservation Services on May 27, 2021; cover shows Commonwealth approval June 2021. It updates the 2013 plan (the first modern plan; the one before it dated from 1983).

**Page numbers are the plan's printed page numbers.** The plan has no action ID scheme, so cite actions by their wording and the goal they sit under (Action Plan, pp. 98-106).

## How to use this file when summarizing meetings

- Connect a meeting item to this plan only when the site, topic or action clearly matches. Say which goal or action it relates to.
- The plan is a state-approved guidance document with a seven-year horizon (through 2028), not a regulation. Do not say a board "must" follow it. Because the 2021-2028 window is closing, a meeting may discuss updating it (the Master Plan lists updating the OSRP as a short-term action, A-045).
- Many actions list several "Project Partners." A partner listed on an action is not automatically the lead. DPW and the Office of Community & Economic Development (OCED) are named on most.
- This file covers goals, objectives, needs, and the main actions. Complete action lists, maps, the ADA transition plan and the Harold King Forest Stewardship Plan are in the full PDF (Appendices IV and V).

---

## 1. Plan basics

- **Purpose:** identify, inventory and prioritize open space and recreation facilities and recommend maintenance, preservation and expansion. COVID-19 usage increases (for example at Linscott Park in summer 2020) shaped the emphasis on protecting multi-functional spaces.
- **Committee:** the OSRPC was formed by the Select Board (then Board of Selectmen) in 2012. It meets monthly and has spun off the Tree Advisory Task Force and the Organic Lawn Maintenance Task Force. 2021 members included the Planning Board chair (Angela Ippolito) and members tied to the Conservation Commission, Historical Commission, Historic District Commission and the Swampscott Conservancy.
- **Process (2020):** a 15-question online survey ran March to August 14, 2020 (about 110 responses on individual questions, plus paper responses at the Farmer's Market); results were presented to the Planning Board (Sept. 14, 2020) and Select Board (Sept. 16, 2020); the draft was open for public comment September to November 2020, with a virtual open house on October 13, 2020, and was sent to boards and committees in October 2020.
- **Structure:** **6 goals, 10 objectives, 77 actions**, with partners among 14 Town boards and committees plus departments.
- **Action timing labels:** Years 1-7, **ONGOING** (multi-year or carried over), **MONITOR** (act if a triggering situation arises). Each action also has a priority (High/Medium/Low) and possible funding sources.
- **Setting facts cited:** the town is about 1,951 acres, small, densely populated and largely built out, so development is mostly redevelopment. About 35% of residents are projected to be 60 or older by 2030.

## 2. The six goals (ranked by community importance in the survey, pp. 84-87, 94)

1. **Maintain Open Spaces & Recreation Facilities:** site-specific maintenance, safety and replacement plans for parks, playgrounds, fields and other amenities; ongoing coordination with DPW, Recreation and Community Development.
2. **Preserve the Scenic Character of the Town:** shade trees and greenscape improvements; build open space and recreation needs into land use planning and zoning; protect historically significant sites; ensure coastal public access is not further restricted by development.
3. **Expand & Improve Open Spaces & Recreation Facilities to Meet Needs:** beach amenities and playground/park accessibility; research and acquire vacant land for open space or recreation.
4. **Strengthen Environmental Protection:** climate-resilient design for coastal open space; protect existing open space from reuse for anything other than open space or recreation.
5. **Improve Public Access & Awareness:** signage, print and online materials, trail maps.
6. **Establish a Green Corridor Network:** connected walking and biking routes between open spaces, recreation facilities, beaches and rail transit, some segments doubling as wildlife passage.

## 3. The ten objectives (pp. 7-8, 94-95)

1. Commit unbuildable and suitable property to open space uses (the plan encourages the Town Administration and Select Board to consider all municipal land).
2. Improve regular maintenance and add capacity and resources (support DPW; coordinate with the Swampscott Conservancy).
3. Increase print and online materials that promote open spaces.
4. Ensure all amenities planned for Town open space align with and comply with OSRP standards (the OSRPC reviews projects).
5. Prioritize redevelopment over greenfield development (infill and redevelopment policies through the land-use boards).
6. Keep improving accessibility at Town-owned open space and recreation (with the Commission on Disability and Swampscott for All Ages).
7. Ensure all-ages open space and recreation options exist throughout Town (with Swampscott for All Ages and Recreation).
8. Connect Green Corridor areas with future streetscape projects for multimodal access (Complete Streets tools; Swampscott adopted a Complete Streets Prioritization Plan in fall 2018).
9. Make all park and open space upgrades sustainable and responsive to climate change.
10. Ensure appropriate protections exist for existing open space (conservation restrictions and similar tools).

## 4. Needs identified (pp. 88-92)

**Resource protection (carried from the prior plan):**
- **Coastal protection:** beaches were one of residents' top three places visited; water quality and erosion are concerns. The Town adopted a Coastal Flood Area Overlay District in 2019. The plan recommends a **local wetlands protection bylaw** to supplement the state Wetlands Protection Act.
- **Freshwater:** three ponds (Foster's, Muskrat, Palmer) and five certified vernal pools; more could be certified.
- **Fisheries and wildlife:** protect forest habitat (Harold A. King Forest, Charles M. Ewing Woods) and marine habitat (harbor eelgrass); support pollinators; connect fragmented habitat.
- **Vegetation:** control invasive species and encourage native plantings.
- **New need:** protect existing amenities and undeveloped land, with equitable replacement of any displaced open space.

**Community needs:**
- **Access for all ages and abilities:** ADA upgrades guided by the Town's ADA Assessment and Transition Plan (funded by a Massachusetts Office on Disability grant; covers Town buildings, Phillips Park and Linscott Park); a "tot lot" for very young children (planned at Windsor Park).
- **Walking, biking and trails:** coordinate sidewalk and bike projects with open space access; priority new paths include the **White Court/Blythswood Easement** and the **Forest River Conservation Area Connector**.
- **Beach amenities:** granite beach entrance markers with dune nourishment and plantings are done at Fisherman's Beach, King's Beach and Eiseman's/New Ocean House Beach; Cassidy Park/Sandy Beach and Phillips Beach entrances were in process. The Harbor and Waterfront Plan recommends restrooms, foot-washing stations and food areas, and larger ideas such as living reefs; the OSRPC wants any beach improvement to protect the resource and withstand climate change.
- **Other:** protect historic sites, keep Town gathering spots in use, maintain parks with benches and amenities.

**Management and maintenance (pp. 92-93):**
- DPW is the primary maintainer (parks, fields, vegetation, beaches, public buildings, 50 miles of streets and sidewalks); school grounds belong to the School Department except winter plowing. DPW contracts out mowing, snow removal and similar work and rakes beaches three days a week in summer.
- The Town has a Tree Warden within DPW; **Fall 2020 Town Meeting adopted a Tree Bylaw.**
- **Foster's Pond** is the only pond with no assigned maintenance owner (DPW maintains the Windsor Avenue dam). Palmer Pond and Muskrat Pond are Town-owned.
- Forests (Ewing Woods, Harold King Forest, Upper Jackson Woods) have been maintained mostly by volunteers; a DCR Forest Stewardship Plan for Harold King Forest produced several action items. Rail Trail and trail upkeep is expected to mix DPW with volunteers (Swampscott Conservancy, Friends of the Swampscott Rail Trail).
- A new action creates **maintenance plans for parks** with DPW and Recreation.

## 5. Selected land inventory (pp. 62-80)

- **Forests, ponds, woods:** Harold A. King Forest (47 acres, conservation use only); Charles M. Ewing Woods (7.3 acres); Palmer Pond (17.75 acres); Muskrat Pond (0.4 acres); Blythswood (6.02 acres).
- **Parks and fields:** Phillips Park (21.3 acres); Jackson Park (30 acres, with Jackson Woods); Middle School recreation area (12.1 acres); Abbott Park (2.1 acres); Linscott Park (2 acres); Town Hall Lawn (3.1 acres); Windsor Park (0.3 acres); Swampscott Cemetery (30 acres).
- **Beaches named:** Fisherman's, King's, Eiseman's/New Ocean House, Whale's, Phillips, Preston, Cassidy Park/Sandy Beach, plus Johnson Park and Chaisson Park as beach-adjacent parks.
- **Quarry:** Aggregate Industries operates a quarry on 210 acres (160 in active mining, about 220 feet deep); the plan seeks public access to Aggregate-owned land, including Foster's Pond and land behind Windsor Park.
- **Machon School:** 1.029 acres next to the High School and Jackson Park; a 2012 Town Meeting vote preserved public path access to Jackson Park Woods.

## 6. Action Plan highlights (pp. 98-106)

**Actions the plan marks High priority (as best the table can be read):**
- Limit the impact of school construction on conservation land and other open space (Ewing Woods, Linscott Park, Phillips Park). Partners: Conservation Commission, School Building Committee. Year 1.
- Repair seawalls and stairways at King's, Eiseman's and Whale's Beach, accounting for sea level rise and storm surge per the Beach Management Plan (FEMA funding). DPW. Years 1-3.
- Beach-appropriate landscaping at Chaisson Park and Johnson Park. Year 2.
- Reduce pesticide use or switch to organic options (DPW, Select Board, Board of Health, Organic Lawn Maintenance Task Force; TURI grants).
- Adopt an **OSRPC policy for Town improvement projects** covering ADA access, amenities for all ages, historically appropriate landscaping, public art, sustainable design, net-neutral energy and natural resource protection. Year 1.
- Add features for small children (tot lot) at Windsor Park and Abbott Park (PARC grant). Years 1 and 4.
- Public access and management plan with interpretive signage for the Blythswood/White Court Easement. Year 1.

**Other notable actions (priority generally Medium or Low):**
- **Land and protection:** dedicate Town-owned open space to permanent protection through easements; acquire a conservation restriction on Jackson Woods; develop policies for acquiring private land (with right of first refusal); create a financing mechanism for land acquisition; **re-evaluate adopting the Community Preservation Act** (Select Board; CPA has since passed, see the Master Plan file); require the Hadley Elementary School site to keep playground or green space of equal or greater area if reused.
- **Land use and environment:** incorporate open space goals into zoning (Planning Board, OCED); local wetlands bylaw (Conservation Commission, Planning Board; Year 3); consider climate change in planning; map areas where zoning should limit development impacts; green infrastructure policy and native planting policy for municipal property; invasive species assessment and control; tree inventory and management plan focused on native trees (Year 4); replacement plan for aging street trees; certify more vernal pools; educate coastal owners on resilience; winter salt alternatives; limit harbor dredging impacts on eelgrass; storm drain work to eliminate bacteria sources at King's Beach.
- **Facilities:** ADA beach access mats (Phillips, Fisherman's and Preston beaches); improve tennis and basketball courts (one-for-one replacement if removed); explore a splash pad, beach activity areas, kayak storage, public showers and restrooms with the Harbor & Waterfront Advisory Committee; improve sidewalks along Humphrey Street.
- **Trails and access:** support the Rail Trail (OCED, DPW, Select Board; ongoing); trails and bird blinds per the Harold King Forest Stewardship Plan; access to Foster's Pond and Aggregate land; Palmer Pond access with the adjacent Temple; maintain the Swampscott Cemetery to Forest River trail connection with Salem.
- **Awareness:** unified signage at facility entrances, web-to-print trail maps, a school education plan, a high school student seat on the OSRPC each year, Essex Heritage scenic byway signage.
- **Green Corridor:** identify the corridor network along streets and easements; community engagement plan and Town-wide walking map; signage with historical elements; wildlife corridors; water bottle refill stations.
- **Historic:** identify and protect historic sites; extend National Register status from the Lynn line to Red Rock at King's Beach (Historical Commission); preserve the Olmsted-designed Monument Avenue planting plan.

**Land acquisition priorities (Map 12):** land abutting Foster Pond, Phillips Beach, land abutting Windsor Park, undeveloped land near Archer Street and the Lynn line. **Conservation restriction priorities:** the forested quarry buffer, Jackson Woods/Upper Jackson Park, private portions of Phillips Beach.

**Funding sources named:** state PARC (Parkland Acquisitions & Renovations for Communities), LWCF (Land & Water Conservation Fund), LAND grants, DCR Forest Stewardship Program, Massachusetts Office on Disability project grants, FEMA Hazard Mitigation Assistance, AARP Livable Communities, Essex Heritage grants, TURI.

## 8. Known quirks

- The action table's priority and year columns are hard to align row by row in the source; where this file gives a priority or year, it comes from the clearest rows. Check the PDF (pp. 99-106) before quoting a specific priority.
- The 2021 plan describes Rail Trail and beach projects as they stood in 2020-2021; later status is in the Master Plan file and in current meeting records.
- The plan cites 1,951 acres for the Town's area, while the housing plan cites 3.58 square miles (which includes water).
