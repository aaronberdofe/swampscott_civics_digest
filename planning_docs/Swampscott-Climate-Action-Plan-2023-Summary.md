# Resilient Swampscott Climate Action & Resilience Plan (2023): Condensed Reference for Meeting Summaries

**Source:** *Resilient Swampscott: Climate Action & Resilience Plan*, prepared by Kim Lundgren Associates, Inc. for the Town of Swampscott (file dated April 24, 2023; a 15-page summary document). The Climate Action Plan Committee oversaw it (committee page: swampscottma.gov/climate-action-plan-committee). It opens with statements from Town Administrator Sean Fitzgerald and committee chair Martha Schmitt. In 2022 the Town passed a resolution to develop a plan and to **reach net zero emissions by 2050**.

**IDs below follow the plan's own numbering:** focus area, then goal number, then strategy (1.1), then action letter (1.1.A). Example: "Buildings & Energy 2.2.A" is the Specialized Energy Code action. The summary document lists no lead departments, costs or timelines for individual actions.

## How to use this file when summarizing meetings

- Connect a meeting item to the plan only when the topic clearly matches an action below. Name the focus area and action ID.
- The plan is guidance. Do not say a board must follow it. Do not claim an action has been completed unless the meeting record says so. The plan reports baselines from 2017-2022, so current numbers may differ.
- Other plans cover related ground: the Master Plan file (Climate, Sustainability, Energy & Hazard Mitigation element; Climate Action & Resilience Committee actions) and the Open Space plan (coastal protection, trees, green infrastructure).

## 1. Framework

- **Three guiding principles:** Community (elevate community voices through meaningful engagement); Equity (equitable access to services and opportunities); GHG Reduction (reduce community-wide greenhouse gas emissions).
- **Two approaches:** reduce emissions, and adapt to impacts already occurring.
- **Five focus areas:** Buildings & Energy; Transportation; Solid Waste & Recycling; Natural Resources; Resilience & Vulnerability. Each has two goals (Solid Waste has one) with strategies and 5 to 8 actions.

## 2. Climate impacts on Swampscott (plan's figures)

- **Heat:** about 2 days a year above 90°F in Essex County on average for 2017-2021; about 29 extreme heat days projected by 2050. Days above 96°F are projected to rise **128.6%** over the next 30 years.
- **Flooding and sea level:** Boston saw about 8 inches of sea level rise 1950-2016; up to 20 inches projected by 2050.
- **Storms and precipitation:** annual precipitation in Essex County rose 9% from 1980 to 2020; another 9% increase is projected for Swampscott by 2050.
- **Exposure:** **1,071 of 4,121 homes (26%)** face moderate flood risk; **7 of 15 social facilities (47%)**, such as schools and government buildings, face major flood risk (Risk Factor flood data, 2023).

## 3. Greenhouse gas emissions

**2017 emissions by sector:** Buildings 52%; on-road transportation 34%; natural gas leaks 8%; solid waste 3%; non-road transportation 2%; wastewater treatment 1%.

**"Pathways to Zero" (modeled path to near zero by 2050):**
- Electrifying 100% of buildings by 2050 delivers about a **45%** reduction in emissions.
- Transportation strategies (vehicle electrification, EV charging, transit, alternative modes) deliver about **34%**.
- Reaching 100% renewable electricity (more Swampscott Community Power participation and about **3,400 new local solar systems** by 2050) delivers about **14%**.
- Cutting waste 90% by 2050 (eliminating about 7,300 tons a year) delivers about **2%**.
- The remaining gap is expected to come from electrifying commercial medium- and heavy-duty trucks.

## 4. Action plan by focus area

### Buildings & Energy
- **Goal 1: Energy infrastructure is emissions-free and supplied by 100% renewable energy.**
  - 1.1 Achieve 100% renewable electricity and cut methane leaks in the gas system. **1.1.A** increase enrollment in **Swampscott Community Power 100% New England Green** through promotion and education. **1.1.B** work with National Grid to prioritize repairing leaking gas pipes under the Massachusetts Gas System Enhancement Plan, coordinate with road paving, and report progress quarterly.
  - 1.2 Maximize local renewable generation and battery storage. **1.2.A** promote solar incentives, prioritizing low-income residents and landlords. **1.2.B** consider a bulk purchasing program for solar and batteries.
- **Goal 2: Buildings are resilient and net-zero.**
  - 2.1 Phased deep energy retrofits and electrification. **2.1.A** multilingual weatherization and efficiency campaign prioritizing low-income residents, renters and landlords. **2.1.B** incentives such as reduced or waived permit fees for projects beyond code (all-electric, renewable).
  - 2.2 New development reduces emissions. **2.2.A** adopt the **Municipal Opt-in Specialized Energy Code**. **2.2.B** adopt the state's **Commercial PACE (C-PACE)** program for local financing of commercial clean energy projects.

### Transportation
- **Goal 1: A safe, accessible, low-emission transportation system.**
  - 1.1 Reduce single-occupancy trips. **1.1.A** expand the bike path and bike-sharing network to connect transit, schools and businesses. **1.1.B** ridesharing/carpooling and income-based fee reduction programs. **1.1.C** shuttle linking commuter rail and the Blue Line to retail hubs and beaches.
  - 1.2 **1.2.A** improve pedestrian and bicycle safety with streetscape features, focusing on high-traffic areas.
- **Goal 2: Electrify cars, trucks and buses.**
  - 2.1 **2.1.A** public awareness campaign for EVs and charging stations. **2.1.B** strengthen the Efficiency and Procurement Policy for Town Vehicles to require buying or leasing EVs for light-duty replacements and pilot medium- and heavy-duty EVs.
  - 2.2 **2.2.A** add EV charging at public parking (examples named: Phillips Park, Fisherman's Beach, Police Station, the new elementary school) and on-street. **2.2.B** train municipal staff on EV maintenance and operations.

### Resilience & Vulnerability
- **Goal 1: Infrastructure and services are resilient to climate impacts.**
  - 1.1 **1.1.A** protect municipal assets (elevate critical equipment, floodproofing). **1.1.B** assess impacts on critical infrastructure and develop a phased upgrade plan.
  - 1.2 **1.2.A** **expand the Coastal Flood Area Overlay District** with a locally regulated floodplain that includes areas outside the FEMA 100-year boundary, based on local data. **1.2.B** **update the Hazard Mitigation Plan** using the latest projections and the Coastal Climate Change Study Final Report.
- **Goal 2: Residents are better prepared for climate change and its health impacts.**
  - 2.1 **2.1.A** inclusive multilingual climate preparedness campaign. **2.1.B** emergency preparedness and response training for staff, first responders and residents. **2.1.C** neighbor-to-neighbor network.

### Natural Resources
- **Goal 1: Natural resources enhance community resilience.**
  - 1.1 **1.1.A** connected network of green spaces to reduce heat and flooding, prioritizing neighborhoods most vulnerable (including heat islands). **1.1.B** landscape practices supporting native biodiversity and water retention. **1.1.C** require removed or lost trees to be replaced with native, climate-resilient species.
- **Goal 2: Infrastructure and open spaces support a healthy, diverse ecosystem.**
  - 2.1 **2.1.A** secure funding for green infrastructure on public property. **2.1.B** place **Conservation Restrictions** on large Town-owned open space parcels. **2.1.C** education campaign on regenerative landscape practices.

### Solid Waste & Recycling
- **Goal 1: Minimize solid waste through reduced consumption and diversion to recycling or composting.**
  - 1.1 **1.1.A** REUSE program (free-exchange or thrift space, enhance the Library of Things, free repair events for seniors and lower-income households). **1.1.B** phased zero-waste education program in Town offices, buildings and schools.
  - 1.2 **1.2.A** mandatory recycling and composting policy for schools and municipal buildings. **1.2.B** outreach to expand resident and business composting participation. **1.2.C** engage local businesses on waste diversion, including diversion-friendly packaging.

## 5. Baselines and targets ("Measuring Success")

| Metric | Baseline | 2030 target | 2050 target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Households electrified | 214 (4%), 2022 | 1,902 (38%) | 5,282 (100%) |
| Installed solar capacity | 2.3 MW, 2022 (1.6 MW in 2017) | 17.8 MW | 35.7 MW (all potential) |
| Households enrolled in New England Green via Swampscott Community Power | 0.3%, 2022 | 80% | 100% |
| Open gas leaks | Grade G3SEI: 6; Grade 3: 85; Grade 2: 13 (2021, HEET leaks map) | Address all G3SEI and Grade 3 | Reduce Grade 2 by 50% |
| EVs as share of registered vehicles | 1%, 2022 | 19% | 100% |
| Transit riders | about 2,000 (2020) | +20% (about 2,400) | +100% (about 4,000) |
| Homes at moderate flood risk | 26%, 2022 | Monitor and reduce | Monitor and reduce |
| Social facilities at major flood risk | 47%, 2022 | Monitor and reduce | Monitor and reduce |
| Heat-related emergency room visits | 2, 2017 | Reduce to 0 | Reduce to 0 |
| Tree canopy cover | 55%, 2016 | Monitor and increase | Monitor and increase |
| Impervious surface | 17%, 2016 | Less than 17% | Less than 17% |
| Total residential waste disposed | 4,015 tons, 2018 | 2,811 tons | 402 tons |
| Household waste generation | 1,612 lbs/year, 2018 | 1,128 lbs | 161 lbs |

**Other facts:**
- Residential energy use is 24% electricity and 76% natural gas and oil. Solar capacity grew 36% from 2017 to 2022.
- Commuting (2020): 63% drive alone, 17% transit, 12% work from home, 6% carpool, 2% walk, 0.3% bike.
- Tree canopy fell 8.2% from 2010, with about 110 acres lost to development.
- In 2022 the Town disposed of 13% less waste than its 2018 baseline; 34% of waste is diverted and 66% (3,468 tons) goes to incineration or landfill.

## 7. Notes and known quirks

- Status cross-checks from the Master Plan file: that plan states the Town adopted the **Specialized Energy Code in 2024** (action 2.2.A) and proposed **Coastal Flood Area Overlay updates for the 2025 Annual Town Meeting** (action 1.2.A), and that MVP 2.0 planning was under way in 2026. Verify the current status in meeting records.
- The plan's commuter table lists "Walking/biking: 23%" in the Measuring Success table, but the chart on the same topic shows 2% walk and 0.3% bike. Treat the walking/biking share as uncertain.
- This is a summary version of the plan. It does not name lead departments, schedules or costs; if a meeting item refers to them, say they are not in this document.
